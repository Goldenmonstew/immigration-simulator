A4.70 Determination of whether a medical waiver should be granted (residence and temporary entry)

  1. Any decision to grant a medical waiver must be made by an immigration officer with Schedule 1-3 delegations (see A15.5).
  2. When determining whether a medical waiver should be granted, an immigration officer must consider the circumstances of the applicant to decide whether they are compelling enough to justify allowing entry to, and/or a stay in New Zealand.
  3. Factors that officers may take into account in making their decision include, but are not limited to, the following:
    1. the objectives of Health instructions (see A4.1) and the objectives of the category or instructions under which the application has been made;
    2. the degree to which the applicant would impose significant costs and/or demands on New Zealand's health or education services;
    3. whether the applicant has immediate family lawfully and permanently resident in New Zealand and the circumstances and duration of that residence;
    4. whether the applicant's potential contribution to New Zealand will be significant;
    5. the length of intended stay (including whether a person proposes to enter New Zealand permanently or temporarily).
  4. For applicants applying for a student or visitor visa as a dependent of a work, student or military visa holder, officers may also take into account whether the applicant's parent’s potential contribution to New Zealand will be significant in making their decision.
  5. Unless the applicant has one of the conditions listed in A4.60(a) or cannot be granted a medical waiver because they were eligible to be included in an earlier residence application as set out at A4.60(b), medical waivers will be granted to applicants who are:
    1. applying for a temporary class visa and can be considered for a waiver because they are a partner or child or a New Zealand citizen or residence class visa holder as set out at A4.65(a)(iv); or
    2. applying for a residence class visa, and are required to be granted a waiver as set out in A4.60(d) because they are a person who would otherwise meet the criteria for residence if they applied for or were included in an application under the Partnership Category (F2) or Dependent Category (F5).
  6. An applicant who has been recognised as having refugee or protection status (except those invited to apply under the Community Organisation Refugee Sponsorship category) will be granted a medical waiver, unless the limitation on the grant of medical waivers to such persons set out at A4.60(a) applies.
  7. An immigration officer should consider any advice provided by an Immigration New Zealand medical assessor on medical matters pertaining to the grant of a waiver, such as the prognosis of the applicant.
  8. An immigration officer must record decisions to approve or decline a medical waiver, and the full reasons for such a decision.
