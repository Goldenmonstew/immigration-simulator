Joint Committee on Justice, Home Affairs and Migration to meet for pre-legislative scrutiny of the General Scheme of the Irish Nationality and Citizenship (Amendment) Bill 2026

19 Nov 2026, 11:50

The Joint Committee on Justice, Home Affairs and Migration will begin its pre-legislative scrutiny of the General Scheme of the Irish Nationality and Citizenship (Amendment) Bill 2026, which proposes significant changes to the requirements for obtaining Irish citizenship through naturalisation. 

The General Scheme was referred to the Committee for pre-legislative scrutiny following Government approval for the priority drafting of the legislation in September 2026.

The first pre-legislative scrutiny meeting will take place today, Thursday November 19th, from 3.30pm to 6pm in Committee Room 2, Leinster House. The Committee will hear from representatives of the Migrant Rights Centre Ireland, Nasc, the Immigrant Council of Ireland, The Open Community and Doras. 
 
The Committee will hold another public meeting on Tuesday next, November 24th and the following have been invited to participate: IBEC; IHREC; Ms Keire Murphy; The Bar of Ireland; Irish Medical Organisation; Nursing Homes Ireland.

The proposals include increasing the qualifying period of reckonable residence for most applicants from five to eight years, introducing language and Irish civics tests, requiring applicants to demonstrate financial self-sufficiency and grounds for the Minister for Justice to revoke citizenship in the case of threats to national security. 
There are also proposed changes to the naturalisation process for the spouses of Irish citizens including increasing the period a foreign-national spouse or civil partner should be residing in the State before being eligible to apply for citizenship. 

Speaking ahead of the meeting, Committee Cathaoirleach Deputy Matt Carthy, said: 

“The proposed legislation represents a significant change to Ireland’s naturalisation framework and raises important questions about access to citizenship, integration, the treatment of different categories of applicants and the practical implications of the proposed new requirements." 

“It is important that we fully examine the rationale for the proposed changes and whether the new requirements are fair, proportionate and workable.” 

“The Committee will hear a range of perspectives, including from organisations working directly with migrants and people seeking citizenship. The Committee welcomes the opportunity to scrutinise the proposals thoroughly and identify any unintended consequences before it progresses further.” 

Watch the meeting live on www.oireachtas.ie or on the Oireachtas smartphone app for Apple and Android.
